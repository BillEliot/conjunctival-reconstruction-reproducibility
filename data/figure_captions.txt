Figure 1. Inference procedures within each fine-model training variant. The original cascade blends regional fine predictions with coarse probabilities. The corrected cascade retains the fine map only when a member proposes exactly one box equal to the image bounds; other proposals use the original reconstruction. Direct fine inference processes the whole photograph at 512 pixels without proposals or coarse/fine blending. Within each procedure, five member probability maps are averaged before thresholding at 0.5. RGB, red–green–blue; px, pixels.

Figure 2. Deterministic synthetic examples of reconstruction for one ensemble member on an 80 × 120 canvas. Columns show the coarse map, original cascade, corrected cascade and direct fine map on the same probability scale. (a) For exactly one whole-image proposal, corrected and direct maps coincide. (b) For a partial proposal, corrected and original maps coincide. Dashed rectangles indicate proposals, inset slightly for visibility; white contours mark probability 0.5. The examples illustrate the reconstruction operation and are not patient images.

Figure 3. Mean image-level Dice for the eight initial configurations on (a) the internal test set (54 patients) and (b) the public dataset (212 photographs). Points and error bars show means and 95% percentile bootstrap confidence intervals from 10,000 resamples of patients or public source records. Gray denotes whole-image baselines; blue denotes fine-model variants evaluated with original cascade reconstruction. F, B, K and C are defined in Table 1. Horizontal-axis ranges differ between panels, and the datasets are not paired.

Figure 4. Reconstruction effects on the public dataset. (a) Mean paired Dice differences and 95% percentile bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs), expressed in percentage points (212 source records). The first two contrasts use identical fine-model weights; the combined-versus-plain contrast compares separately fitted loss variants. (b) Mean Dice with descriptive 95% CIs for original, corrected and direct combined-model inference, partitioned by proposal geometry. All five members proposed exactly one whole-image region in 125 photographs; at least one member proposed a partial region in the other 87. CIs use 10,000 within-subgroup source-record resamples. Corrected and direct probability arrays are identical in the whole-image subgroup. The subgroups are disjoint and are not paired with each other.

Figure 5. Real-image comparisons on Eyes-defy-anemia [5,6]. Columns show the photograph, source reference, original cascade, corrected cascade and direct fine inference using the combined-model checkpoints. Cyan dashed contours denote reference boundaries; yellow solid contours denote predicted boundaries. Each row uses one shared display crop without color adjustment. Dice values are calculated on the complete reference canvas. Examples were selected before high-resolution inspection by proximity to a subgroup median: (a) corrected-minus-original gain among 103 improving whole-image records; (b) direct-minus-corrected gain among 73 improving partial-proposal records; (c) corrected-minus-original decline among 22 declining whole-image records; (d) direct Dice among seven records with empty original and corrected cascade predictions. Source-record order resolved ties. Corrected and direct probabilities are identical in (a) and (c). These examples illustrate the observed effects and their limits; cohort-level comparisons are reported in Tables 3 and 4. Source photographs and references: Eyes-defy-anemia, Dimauro et al. [6], CC BY 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Display crops and prediction contours were added.
